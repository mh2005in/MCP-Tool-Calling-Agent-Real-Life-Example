1. Executive Summary

The application has a credible domain-specific core. It already goes beyond a generic client database by supporting case
intake, conditional checklists, document review, expiry alerts, consistency checks, consultant approvals, travel and work
histories, relationship evidence, LMIA recruitment evidence, client checklists, and third-party portals. Its Microsoft Entra
identity architecture and AI boundary controls also create a serious foundation for regulated work.

           Overall finding: the platform is strong as a case-preparation workflow system, but it is not yet complete
           enough to replace the primary practice-management stack used by an immigration firm. The largest gaps
           are IRCC form automation, money movement, electronic signatures, calendar and communications, full
           portal capabilities, team task management, and production-grade document operations.

 Top recommendations

s Make IRCC package automation the product's central workflow: versioned forms, field mapping, validation, package
     assembly, and filing readiness.

s Close the commercial workflow with invoicing, payments, trust-account controls, retainers, and electronic signatures - using
     integrations where regulated or commodity infrastructure already exists.

s Create one operational timeline for tasks, deadlines, appointments, communications, document events, payments, and
     approvals.

s Upgrade the client experience from token-based task access to a secure, multilingual portal with messages, files,
     appointments, signatures, invoices, and status visibility.

s Ship security, privacy, data portability, content governance, monitoring, and recovery capabilities alongside revenue features
     rather than postponing them.

Key product constraint

           The present product is optimized around preparing a case correctly. Buyers, however, evaluate the
           entire firm lifecycle: acquire the lead, book the consultation, retain and bill the client, collect evidence,
           prepare forms, communicate, file, monitor, report, and close/archive the matter. Missing links in that chain
           create duplicate work and reduce willingness to switch from an incumbent.

 4. Detailed Missing Features and Recommendations

 4.1 IRCC Form and Submission-Package Automation

  PRIORITY P0 | ESTIMATED EFFORT XL | DELIVERY STRATEGY: BUILD THE ORCHESTRATION; LICENSE OR INTEGRATE PDF
  TECHNOLOGY

 Observed gap

  No complete workflow was identified for populating official application forms, controlling form editions, validating form-specific
  rules, producing barcodes where applicable, or assembling a submission-ready package.

 Required capabilities

s Maintain a versioned catalogue of federal and provincial forms with effective dates, source URLs, hashes, retirement dates,
     and affected programs.

s Map normalized intake answers to reusable person, address, education, employment, travel, family, and immigration-history
     fields.

s Populate supported PDF fields while preserving official form behavior; detect unsupported or altered source files.
s Run cross-form validation: names, dates, passport details, addresses, family composition, gaps, signatures, and

     program-specific requirements.
s Generate a filing-readiness report separating errors, warnings, consultant decisions, client confirmations, and unresolved

     evidence.
s Assemble a package index with document order, naming conventions, size limits, translations, certified copies, and

     case-specific notes.
s Require explicit consultant approval before final package generation; retain the form version, mapping version, inputs, output

     hash, and approver in the audit trail.

              Recommendation: Begin with the 3-5 highest-volume Canadian programs and a canonical applicant data
              model. Treat forms as governed product content, not hard-coded UI. Do not automate final portal
              submission until reliability, terms of use, and operational support are proven.

 Minimum viable release

  One-click generation for a limited program set; version registry; mapping review screen; deterministic validation; package
  index; consultant sign-off; generated-file audit record.

 Success measures

  Field reuse above 70%; preparation time reduced 30-50%; less than 1% mapping defects detected after approval; 100%
  outputs linked to a form version and approver.

 Key dependencies

  Canonical data model, content-release workflow, PDF engine, secure generated-file storage, validation rules, audit log,
  regression fixtures.

 4.2 Billing, Payments, Trust Accounting, and Financial Controls

  PRIORITY P0 | ESTIMATED EFFORT L | DELIVERY STRATEGY: BUY/INTEGRATE PAYMENTS; BUILD IMMIGRATION-SPECIFIC
  BILLING WORKFLOW

 Observed gap

  No invoice, payment, ledger, expense, time-entry, refund, receipt, or trust-account entities were identified. A case can record
  retainer status but not the financial lifecycle.

 Required capabilities

s Quotes, fee schedules, invoices, taxes, discounts, disbursements, credit notes, receipts, refunds, and write-offs.
s Milestone and installment plans tied to case stage, with reminders and overdue status.
s Hosted online payments through a compliant processor; never store raw card data.
s Trust versus operating funds, client-level ledgers, transfers, reconciliation support, immutable adjustments, and exportable

     audit history.
s Time and expense capture where firms use hourly or hybrid billing.
s Accounting exports or synchronization with QuickBooks/Xero and configurable chart-of-account mappings.
s Role separation for creating, approving, refunding, and reconciling financial transactions.

              Recommendation: Integrate a payment provider and accounting platform rather than building payment
              rails or a general ledger. Build the case milestones, fee plan, client ledger views, controls, and audit trail
              that are specific to immigration practice.

 Minimum viable release

  Fee plan, invoices, hosted payment link, receipts, installment status, accounting export, role-controlled refunds, and basic
  client ledger. Defer full trust reconciliation until requirements are validated with licensed practitioners and bookkeepers.

 Success measures

  Invoice-to-payment time; online payment adoption; overdue balance; reconciliation exceptions; percentage of retained cases
  with complete fee plan.

 Key dependencies

  Firm legal entity and tax settings, payment processor, accounting integration, permission model, notification delivery,
  retention policy.

 4.4 Calendar, Consultation Booking, and Deadline Management

  PRIORITY P1 | ESTIMATED EFFORT M | DELIVERY STRATEGY: INTEGRATE EXTERNAL CALENDARS; BUILD LEGAL DEADLINE
  CONTROLS

 Observed gap

  Case deadlines and scheduled reminders exist, but no appointment model, availability rules, calendar view, external calendar
  synchronization, conflict handling, or deadline calculation engine was identified.

 Required capabilities

s Public and invitation-only booking pages with service, duration, location, intake prerequisites, payment requirement, and staff

s Two-way Microsoft 365 and Google Calendar synchronization with conflict prevention and timezone handling.

 Minimum viable release

  overdue escalation.

 Success measures

  No-show rate; missed deadlines; booking conversion; schedule conflicts; overdue items; consultant utilization.

 Key dependencies

  User availability, notification delivery, task engine, external OAuth consent, timezone normalization, audit logging.

 4.5 Unified Communications and Correspondence Record

  PRIORITY P1 | ESTIMATED EFFORT L | DELIVERY STRATEGY: INTEGRATE DELIVERY CHANNELS; BUILD THE CASE
  COMMUNICATION TIMELINE

 Observed gap

  The application can draft and approve reminders and includes mail configuration, but a complete two-way inbox, delivery
  record, reply capture, bounce handling, consent management, and case correspondence timeline were not identified.

 Required capabilities

s Send and receive email within a case; associate replies and attachments automatically or through staff review.
s SMS and WhatsApp through approved providers with opt-in/opt-out, templates, delivery status, and channel fallback.
s Shared firm inbox, assignment, internal notes, mentions, tags, service targets, and collision controls.
s Reusable message templates by language, program, case stage, missing item, and party role.
s Immutable correspondence log with sender, recipient, channel, status, related case/task/document, and approval

     provenance.
s Safe AI drafting with source context, sensitive-data boundaries, consultant review, and no autonomous legal advice.

              Recommendation: Make communication a first-class case object. Keep provider adapters separate from
              message records so channel vendors can change without losing history.

 Minimum viable release

  Outbound email, templates, approval, delivery/bounce status, reply capture, attachments, case timeline, and client
  communication preferences.

 Success measures

  Response time; delivery failure rate; time spent locating correspondence; template reuse; percentage of messages linked to
  a case.

 Key dependencies

  Email/SMS providers, webhook ingestion, portal identity, consent records, retention policy, antivirus scanning.

 4.7 Production Document Management, OCR, and Evidence Operations

  PRIORITY P1 | ESTIMATED EFFORT L | DELIVERY STRATEGY: BUILD WORKFLOW; INTEGRATE STORAGE, OCR, AND MALWARE
  SCANNING

 Observed gap

  Uploads, metadata, status review, and classification exist, but the inspected surface does not show preview/download
  operations, versions, OCR search, duplicates, annotations, redaction, retention disposition, archive, or malware controls.

 Required capabilities

s Secure object storage with tenant/case isolation, encryption, checksums, signed URLs, lifecycle tiers, backup, and recovery
     testing.

s Browser preview, download, rotate, merge/split, page reorder, annotation, redaction, and replacement/version history.
s OCR and indexed search across filename, metadata, and extracted text; language detection and low-confidence review.

s Retention holds, archive, deletion approval, disposition certificate, and complete access/download audit.

              Recommendation: Separate immutable original files from derived previews, OCR text, redactions, and
              assembled packages. Never overwrite the evidentiary original.

 Minimum viable release

  Cloud object storage, preview/download, immutable versions, OCR, text search, malware scan, duplicate warning, access
  log, and package export.

 Success measures

  Search success; average review time; duplicate rate; infected files blocked; storage cost per case; recovery test success.

 Key dependencies

  Blob storage, key management, OCR service, search index, antivirus service, retention policy, object-level permissions.

 4.13 Multi-Tenant SaaS, Firm Administration, and Subscription
 Operations

  PRIORITY P2 | ESTIMATED EFFORT XL | DELIVERY STRATEGY: DESIGN NOW; PHASE IMPLEMENTATION WITH
  COMMERCIALIZATION

 Observed gap

  Organization dashboards and consultant scoping exist, but the inspected model does not demonstrate complete tenant
  isolation, firm-level configuration, office hierarchy, subscription entitlements, usage metering, provisioning, data residency, or
  tenant export/deletion.

 Required capabilities

s Hard tenant boundary on every record, query, cache key, file path, job, event, export, and log context.
s Firm, office, team, user, role, permission, service catalogue, branding, numbering, timezone, locale, and retention settings.
s Subscription plans, trials, seats, storage, usage, add-ons, entitlements, invoices, grace periods, suspension, and reactivation.
s Self-service user invitation, role changes, offboarding, ownership transfer, domain verification, and security-policy settings.
s Tenant backup/restore, export, legal hold, closure, deletion workflow, and evidence of disposition.

              Recommendation: Make tenant ID structurally unavoidable in repositories and storage. Add automated
              cross-tenant security tests before onboarding unrelated firms. Do not rely only on consultant IDs in URLs
              for isolation.

 Minimum viable release

  Firm tenant, scoped repositories/storage, firm admin, roles, invitations, plan entitlements, usage counters, tenant export, and
  automated isolation tests.

 Success measures

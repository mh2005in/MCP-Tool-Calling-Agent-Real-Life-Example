Application Type Deep Dive: Document Collection, Automation Boundaries, and MVP
Requirements

I'll treat this as a requirements/business-domain deep dive, not legal advice. I'll use current official IRCC/government sources for
document-list structure and then translate that into software requirements and automation boundaries.

Important: this is not immigration or legal advice. For the product, the system should always treat IRCC/ESDC official pages,
latest forms, and the consultant's professional judgment as the source of truth. The software should collect, organize, remind,
summarize, and flag missing items, but it should not decide eligibility or tell the client what strategy to choose.

1. Core compliance principle for the product

For every permit/application type, your product should follow this rule:

The system may automate intake, document collection, document tracking, reminders, summaries, and draft
communications. It must not provide immigration advice, eligibility decisions, legal interpretation, or guarantee
outcomes.

This matters because immigration consultants are regulated professionals. Your software should support the consultant, not
replace them.

Area                 Requirement
Source of truth      Link checklist templates to official IRCC/ESDC source pages
Human approval       Consultant must approve checklist before sending to client
Versioning           Store checklist version, source URL, and date reviewed

No legal advice      AI must not recommend program choice or eligibility result
Audit trail          Track who changed checklist, who approved it, and when

3. Study Permit

A study permit allows a foreign national to study at a designated learning institution in Canada. IRCC study permit guidance
includes documents such as a letter of acceptance, proof of identity, proof of financial support, and in many cases a provincial or
territorial attestation letter. For Quebec studies, applicants generally need a CAQ rather than the regular PAL/TAL process.

8. LMIA

LMIA is primarily an employer-side process through Employment and Social Development Canada / Service Canada under the
Temporary Foreign Worker Program. ESDC assesses whether the job offer and business are genuine and legitimate. This is a
premium, high-risk workflow because business legitimacy, recruitment evidence, stream-specific requirements, and timing
matter.

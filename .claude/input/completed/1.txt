AI Intake & Document Follow-up Automation

 Stage 1: Business Domain Understanding and Immigration Consultant Workflow

Scope: from the important product boundary through the recommended next deep dive into Study Permit
workflow and document collection.

1. Important boundary for this product

This product should not decide eligibility, recommend immigration programs, calculate legal strategy, or
tell clients what to do.

In Canada, paid immigration advice and representation is restricted to authorized representatives such as
lawyers, Quebec notaries, and licensed immigration consultants in good standing with the College of
Immigration and Citizenship Consultants. IRCC lists RCICs as authorized paid representatives.

Therefore, this product should position itself as:

Client intake, document collection, follow-up, and file organization automation for
immigration consultants - with human review and consultant approval.

It should not be positioned as:

AI immigration advisor.

That distinction is critical.

2. Typical client journey

Most immigration consultants follow some version of this flow:

Lead inquiry
-> Pre-screening / basic intake
-> Consultation or eligibility discussion
-> Retainer / service agreement
-> Detailed intake form
-> Personalized document checklist
-> Document collection
-> Document review and missing item follow-up
-> Forms/application preparation
-> Client review/signatures
-> Submission through correct IRCC portal
-> Post-submission follow-up
-> Additional document requests / updates
-> Decision / closure / next-step service

Different immigration programs require different forms and instructions, and applicants may need to
submit multiple forms together depending on the program. IRCC also maintains online portals for
authorized representatives, including the APR portal and PR portal, used for different application types.

For this product, the highest-value area is not submission itself. It is the messy middle:

3. What happens at each workflow stage

Stage A - Lead inquiry

Clients usually come from WhatsApp, phone, referral, website form, Facebook, Instagram, Google Business
Profile, or community groups.

The inquiry is often vague, such as: "Can I apply for PR?", "Can you help with study permit?", "My work
permit is expiring soon?", "I got refusal, what can I do?", or "I want to sponsor my spouse."

Area                                    Example questions
Identity                                Name, location, country of citizenship
Status                                  Inside Canada or outside Canada
Current situation                       Visitor, student, worker, out of status, spouse abroad
Goal                                    Study, work, PR, sponsorship, visitor visa, citizenship
Urgency                                 Expiry date, deadline, refusal date, invitation deadline
Contact                                 Email, phone, WhatsApp
Language preference                     English, Punjabi, Hindi, Urdu, French, etc.

Automation opportunity: capture the inquiry, classify service type, ask missing basic questions, and
prepare a consultant-facing lead summary.

Do not automate: "You are eligible for X program."

Stage C - Consultation / eligibility review

This is usually a paid or free call. The consultant asks deeper questions and explains possible options. This

stage is legally sensitive. The software should support the consultant, not replace them.

Possible automation:

Before call:
- collect intake answers
- organize documents already uploaded
- summarize client timeline
- highlight missing facts

After call:
- generate internal notes draft
- draft follow-up email
- create task list
- generate document checklist draft for consultant approval

Professional conduct guidance emphasizes that service agreements should clearly explain the process,
eligibility requirements, and necessary documents for the program involved. This means document
expectations and process clarity are already part of the consultant workflow.

Stage D - Retainer / service agreement

Before full representation, consultants typically need a formal service agreement or retainer. Client-file
rules indicate that a licensee opens a client file when the retainer agreement is signed.

For the product, this creates two phases:

Lead / prospect record
-> becomes client file after retainer signed

This distinction matters for the data model, compliance, access control, and audit trail.

MVP implication: do not overbuild retainer generation at first. Instead, allow the consultant to mark:

Lead status: New / Pre-screened / Consultation booked / Retainer sent / Client retained /
Closed

Later, the product can integrate e-signature and templates.

Stage G - Document collection

This is where consultants waste a lot of time.

client sends documents in WhatsApp
client sends photos instead of scans
file names are unclear
documents are expired
documents are incomplete
documents are in the wrong language
documents are too large
client forgets what is still missing
consultant repeatedly follows up manually
assistant cannot tell file readiness quickly

Some IRCC upload contexts accept formats such as PDF, JPG, TIFF, PNG, DOC, or DOCX, and apply file size
limits. This suggests a useful product feature:

File validation:
- accepted format
- file size warning
- expiry date detection
- document type classification
- naming convention
- missing required item

The product should not submit to IRCC automatically. It should prepare a clean, organized package for the
consultant.

Stage I - Application preparation

The consultant prepares forms and portal entries. For example, Express Entry PR applicants fill out the
application, upload documents, pay fees, and submit the application. Authorized representatives use
different representative portals depending on application type.

This product should not try to replace IRCC portals in the MVP. Instead, it should help with:

- client data completeness
- document readiness
- standardized file names
- internal checklist
- consultant notes
- export package

Later SaaS versions could support structured form-data exports, but portal automation is risky and may
break if IRCC changes flows.

Stage J - Post-submission follow-up

After submission, clients often ask whether there is any update, whether IRCC received the application,
what to do with a new passport, how to update an address, or what to do when IRCC requests another
document.

IRCC provides web forms and account flows for submitting additional documents or updates in certain
scenarios. Product opportunity:

Post-submission tracker:
- application submitted date
- application number
- portal used
- biometrics status
- medical status
- additional document request
- deadline
- client update request
- consultant follow-up tasks

4. The biggest pain points

Pain point 1 - Unstructured client communication

Clients use WhatsApp, email, SMS, phone calls, and random attachments. Impact: the consultant loses track
of what was sent.

Product response: secure client portal, upload links, and checklist-based document collection.

Pain point 2 - Repeated follow-ups

Consultants repeatedly ask for the same missing documents. Impact: low-value admin work.

Product response: automatic reminders for missing checklist items.

Pain point 3 - Poor document quality

Common problems include blurry photos, cropped passports, expired passports, wrong documents,
missing translations, wrong file formats, files that are too large, and documents that are not named
properly.

Product response: document quality checks and consultant review queue.

Pain point 4 - Clients do not understand checklist wording

A client may not know what proof of relationship, proof of funds, or employment reference letter means.

Product response: consultant-approved plain-language explanations. These explanations must be generic
and not case-specific legal advice.

Pain point 5 - Consultants lack quick file visibility

Consultants need to know which files are ready, which clients are stuck, which documents are missing,
which deadlines are close, and which assistant needs to follow up.

Product response: dashboard by case status, missing documents, upcoming deadlines, and unread client
replies.

7. First domain conclusion

The best starting wedge is not a full immigration CRM.

The best starting wedge is:

AI-assisted intake and document follow-up for small Canadian immigration consultants

The product/service helps them:

collect client information
generate consultant-reviewed checklists
collect documents securely
detect missing/incorrect documents
send follow-up reminders
prepare clean case files
track deadlines

It should not decide immigration options or replace the consultant.

8. Recommended first niche

For the MVP, do not start with every immigration service type. Pick one or two high-volume, document-
heavy workflows.

Niche                   Why it is good                        Complexity
Study permit            High volume, document-heavy,          Medium
Spousal sponsorship     many consultants serve this market    High
Visitor visa            Very document-heavy, lots of follow-  Low-medium
Express Entry post-ITA  up, strong pain point                 Medium-high
Work permit / LMIA      Common, simpler checklist, quick      High
                        turnaround
                        Strong document checklist need,
                        deadline pressure
                        Valuable but employer-side

Recommendation for the first pilot: start with study permit + visitor visa, then expand to spousal
sponsorship.

Study permit and visitor visa cases are common, easier to standardize, and good for testing intake,
checklist, upload, reminder, and document review workflows. Spousal sponsorship is attractive but more
complex because relationship evidence can be highly case-specific.

10. Service-business-first version

Before building a full SaaS, sell this as a managed automation service.

Example offer:

We automate your immigration client intake and document follow-up process in 7 days.

You manually configure:

Google Forms / Typeform / Fillout
Airtable / Notion / Google Sheets
Google Drive folder structure
Zapier / Make automations

This lowers risk because you learn the real workflow before spending months building.
